# Innovation–Legislature research dialogue (proposed operating protocol)

Standing Antonina board hub: `GT2QzYsFG4t92DvJkE4Wuk-lodge` ("Viche Innovation–Legislature Dialogue (standing hub)"). This is a **discussion channel**, not a bill registry, legislative sponsorship, a new law, or a constitutional amendment. It gives effect to Act 25's existing permission to report findings to Parliament and O7's Clerk uptake tracking; adoption of any new legal duties remains for Parliament.

## Division of responsibility

- The Executive Innovation Office discovers ideas for **system-wide efficiency and reliability**, and studies current **problems and opportunities** with equal intellectual seriousness. It studies institutions, coordination, technology, architecture, organizational alternatives, and the possibility of simplifying, replacing, or repealing rules. Its outputs are research inquiries, hypotheses, competing possibilities, evidence, and criticism — **not bills or enacted law**.
- The Legislature owns formal bill origination, sponsorship, hearings, statutory text, voting, enactment, and changes to the Judiciary's methods. It can commission further Centre research and decide not to sponsor.
- The Judiciary independently investigates violations, audits compliance, and determines independent factual findings. The Centre does **not** monitor whether particular officers obey existing laws, grade individual violations, duplicate random case sampling, or replace judicial investigations. It may ask whether a law is worth keeping and use public Judiciary findings as *research input* without revising verdicts.
- The Executive implements only measures authorized by existing or subsequently enacted law. Neither a board comment nor an Office investigation authorizes deployment.

## Durable two-way dialogue

1. Either the Office or a legislative member can initiate a **topic-specific Antonina board issue**, with title `Viche inquiry: <question or opportunity>`. No finished solution, detailed legislative draft, or verified defect is required. A vague observation or potentially valuable opportunity is enough.
2. Add the issue ID and a one-line description in a comment to the standing hub. The topic issue is the durable, chronological conversation; use replies on **that same issue** for questions, objections, new evidence, and revised ideas. Do not create new issues just for additional rounds. Read *all pages* when necessary.
3. The Clerk considers open threads when forming the Legislature's agenda, makes a concrete request to a member when collaboration is worthwhile, and sends questions back to the Office by commenting on the topic issue. The Office checks unanswered parliamentary questions during its research cycle. Neither office needs a summons to research or ask a question; however, only parliamentary seats can handle bill sponsorship.
4. Any parliamentary member may ask for more research, challenge assumptions, suggest alternatives, decline consideration with reasons, or independently sponsor a formal bill. There is **no prescribed number of rounds**, no compulsory sponsor, and no automatic conversion from inquiry to bill.
5. Preserve unresolved questions, evidence links, and the reason for a substantive disposition in the topic. O7's living log can link these dispositions. Avoid duplicated status notices or mandatory reply counts. A topic stays open if useful research or legislative discussion remains; lack of immediate action is not approval.
6. After a law is genuinely enacted, the Executive owns implementation and independent effects are checked under existing law. The Office can study whether the law improved system-level performance and bring new opportunities or criticism back through this channel.

## Research breadth and meaningful parallelism

For consequential inquiries, the Office should normally dispatch **parallel, orthogonal** Antonina-managed researchers to separate, isolated worktrees when there are distinct useful questions and adequate resources. Give them genuinely different angles (e.g., organizational redesign, alternative technology or Free Software, reliability failure modes, opportunity discovery, repeal/simplification, independent skeptical critique). Avoid multiple agents repeating the same proposal, and do not invent busywork or impose fixed numerical counts.

An evidence-based claim of breadth should show **what different hypotheses, disciplines, opportunities, organizational models, and simpler baselines were actually examined**, by whom, what challenged the preferred view, and what remained unexamined. Internal reasoning cannot be proven; agent count, reports, legal draft count, or token volume are not proof. Brevity is welcome where the evidence is strong. Research should not be confined to responding to the last incident; create substantive new inquiry threads when an opportunity is genuinely promising.

The Centre is not required to author an implementation plan. Its success is judged by the *eventual usefulness of institutional changes*, verified independently, not by how many ideas or laws are produced.

## Operating boundary

This file describes an implementation proposal and existing-law research communication; it does not itself amend Acts 15 or 25. A pending legislative bill may refine these responsibilities. The Founder-controlled constitutional amendment is a separate deployment, not accomplished by this document.
