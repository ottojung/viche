## Conclusion

The better reading of Act 17 §2.4, in force when the October 23 corrections were filed, is that its one-correction cap applied per case within an audit cycle, and the ordinary six-hour frozen sample was the most concrete cycle boundary then documented. The strongest contrary reading is that six hours was only the ordinary dispatch cadence, while “cycle” could mean a distinct audit pass/dispatch; the statute did not then expressly define cycle identity or prohibit a new correction after a later incident-driven pass. The later frozen-draw identity guard resolves that operational ambiguity prospectively; its express non-retroactivity bars using its token test as if it had already governed.

For both older cases, two correction filings fall within one six-hour interval and cite the same draw (`1792737985`). This is substantial evidence for a same-cycle cap violation under the better reading, but not conclusive proof: the filings lack cycle IDs, the historical rule did not expressly equate a draw with the legal cycle, and the exact contemporaneous cycle/dispatch records are not preserved in the cited JSONLs. I therefore find an **unresolved legal/evidentiary ambiguity**, not an established breach or exoneration. This is not a guilt finding.

## Governing rule and competing readings

Current ordinary law is read from `/var/lib/lubko/wd/viche/government/LAWS.md`. Act 17 §§2.1–2.6.1 permits a superseding verdict on materially stronger/reliable new evidence; makes the Git-committed correction trail authoritative; preserves historical findings and distinct signals; and states “one correction per case per cycle,” requiring stronger evidence and forbidding a bare re-flip (LAWS.md §154–161). The contemporaneous operational description in `government/OVERSIGHT.md` §§35–46 describes an ordinary six-hour audit cycle, with incident checks potentially every 30 minutes reusing the current frozen sample until six hours old. This is strong contemporaneous context for treating a repeated correction during that sample as within the same cycle.

**Best argument for one frozen six-hour cycle:** the limit attaches to the case and “cycle,” not to a session or correction opportunity; allowing each incident re-dispatch to start a new cycle would defeat finality and permit serial flips within a sample. The oversight process already reused the current draw between ordinary six-hour refreshes, making that draw the practical stable unit.

**Counterargument:** the enacted words do not define “cycle,” and operational sampling cadence is not necessarily the legal correction cycle. The law could mean one correction in each audit pass/cycle, so a genuinely new later pass might permit another correction even if an old frozen draw is reused. Missing cycle IDs and the absence of complete dispatch identity evidence prevent proving exactly how the historical actors understood or recorded the relevant cycle. A shared draw is persuasive evidence, not a statutory definition by itself.

The later `government/AUDIT-CYCLE-IDENTITY.md` §§3–21 makes draw timestamp/seed/case IDs and a stable token the explicit identity, reuses that identity for incident checks, and enforces one correction per case/token. Its §§43–49 call this prospective and expressly reserve the October 23 history for separate legal resolution. Applying that token requirement retroactively would therefore be unjustified. It is evidence of a later conservative implementation, not authoritative proof of the former rule's exact scope.
