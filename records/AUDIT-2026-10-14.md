No statutory mechanism exists for:
- Auditing model compliance

- Penalizing violations
- Defining enforcement procedures

**Recommendation:** Amend Act 4 to include model-compliance auditing within the independent audit function's mandate.

## 3. Legislative Integrity

### 3.1 Act 9 vs. Article I — CONSTITUTIONAL TENSION

Article I establishes "high-quality resolution of Antonina board tasks" as the top priority. Act 9 narrows this to "exclusively Viche and Kawun." While Act 9 frames this as the Founder's explicit scope restriction, the Constitution does not mention Kawun or any exclusive scope. The constitutional mechanism for changing priorities is a Founder communication on the Antonina board — but Act 9 is embedded in ordinary legislation.

**Assessment:** Act 9 functions as a de facto constitutional amendment through ordinary legislation. This creates a structural tension with Article IX: "Constitutional constraints prevail over ordinary law."

### 3.2 Act 6.3 vs. Act 9.1 — INTERNAL CONTRADICTION

- Act 6.3: "Ordinary laws may be repealed or rewritten—including the voting rules in this Act—consistent with the Constitution."
- Act 9.1: "Only a future Founder communication... may broaden or narrow this project scope."

If Act 9 is ordinary law, parliament can repeal it under Act 6.3. But repealing Act 9 would broaden scope, which Act 9.1 forbids. The laws do not resolve whether Act 9 is entrenched or ordinary.

### 3.3 Act 9.4 — EXTRACONSTITUTIONAL ENUMERATION

Act 9.4 lists forbidden projects: "Antonina itself, AssemblyP1, QAI, Skrynia, Lubko, Volodyslav, Along or Supernatural." None appear in the Constitution. While this may reflect Founder intent, the Constitution provides no basis for parliament to permanently prohibit work on specific named projects.

## 7. Recommendations

### Priority 1 — Codify Article V Enforcement

Add to Act 4 (Research and audit) a provision requiring the independent audit function to verify model compliance. The Constitution states "mere words do not enforce credential restrictions" — without statutory backing, the model restriction relies entirely on agent self-policing.

### Priority 2 — Resolve Act 9 Constitutional Tension

Either:
- (a) Obtain a recorded Founder communication explicitly authorizing the exclusive Viche/Kawun scope, so Act 9 is properly implementing legislation; or
- (b) Amend Act 9 to explicitly state it is Founder-entrenched and not subject to parliamentary repeal under Act 6.3; or
- (c) Repeal Act 9 and rely on the Constitution's broad mandate, letting the Founder communicate scope preferences through the Antonina board.

### Priority 3 — Fix sitting.sh Clerk Identification

Replace the hardcoded first-row clerk identification with a role-based lookup. Either add a role column to seats.tsv or use a separate configuration file.

### Priority 4 — Exercise the Accountability Infrastructure

The five-day/ten-minute review mechanism (Act 5.3, Article VII) remains aspirational. The parliament should begin producing daily accounts and exercising the voting system.

### Priority 5 — Document Constitutional Mount Path

Reconcile the Constitution's reference to `/constitution/CONSTITUTION.md` with the actual mount at `/var/lib/lubko/from-host/viche-constitution`.

## 8. Conclusion

The Viche government is architecturally sound and largely compliant with the Constitution. The most significant gaps are:

1. The model restriction (Article V) lacks statutory enforcement
2. Act 9 creates a constitutional tension that should be resolved
3. The accountability infrastructure has not been exercised

These are expected findings for a freshly bootstrapped system. The priority is to begin exercising the institutions — holding votes, producing daily accounts, processing Founder communications — while addressing the structural gaps identified in this audit.
