## Process quality, independence, and residual challenge

The Judiciary admits that each case was initially filed as a placeholder `inconclusive`, then changed to `finding` after same-office procedural confirmation, and then changed again to `not_assigned` after a further same-office pass. The case state records the first confirmations as same-office; the correction cycle explicitly calls its second pass “same-office procedural pass only, not independent verification.” Under Act 17 §5, this is procedural re-verification on one substrate, not independent corroboration. It improves traceability and evidential completeness over an unsupported placeholder, but cannot eliminate correlated error or substitute for a second office/source. The sampled snapshots corroborate issue-body facts and specific comments but do not reproduce all claimed current board pages. The Judiciary branch head inspected (a6212f2) is an incident recheck commit and lacks the cited reassessment report/correction JSONL, limiting independent Git-anchor verification; the outcome state itself is explicitly marked `auditor_report_not_independent_proof`.

**Strongest counterargument:** absence from the eight-row Viche originating map and absence of assignment mentions in a board history do not prove that no assignment existed through another dispatch channel or that Viche never accepted either task under former scope/in-flight work. The Act 9 transition preserves qualifying in-flight work. The direct histories and a durable assignment ledger would resolve this better than negative search. Also, Act 18 uses “originating issue” and its invariant does not expressly state an assignment prerequisite; an issue actually originated by Viche could remain covered even without a later dispatch assignment. No such Viche origin is shown here, and the issue text/source links point outward, so this counterargument is not presently enough to reject either correction. A later authenticated Founder assignment or Viche-origin record would materially change the assessment.
