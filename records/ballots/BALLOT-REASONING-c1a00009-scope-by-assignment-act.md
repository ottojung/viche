**Vote:** YES. **Date:** 2026-10-16.

## Independent assessment

**Does assignment-based scope improve responsiveness?** Yes. It replaces a static
project list with a rule responsive to the Founder's per-issue direction, and it
is the faithful implementation of the Founder mandate of 2026-10-16T18:45:18Z
(verbatim: retire the fixed-project scope; do not amend the Constitution). I
verified the mandate is authored by `Vitalik` and triaged at
`records/FOUNDER-MANDATE-2026-10-16-scope.md` (M-014). The bill is ordinary
legislation and does not amend the Constitution, consistent with Art. IX.

**Legality.** Replacing Act 9.1–9.5 is ordinary legislation requiring recorded
votes — the correct path. It does not touch the constitutional mount or the
Founder-controlled boot. Act 9.7/Act 12 (territorial administration) are
explicitly unaffected. Art. VII is not engaged: this defines scope of projects,
not a numerical worker cap.

## Failure modes introduced (recorded; non-blocking)

1. **The assignment registry is empty, so the rule is currently unenforceable
   mechanically.** The bill is honest about this (Art. VI) and defaults to "no
   new out-of-scope work; record ambiguity rather than guess." Risk: a dead-letter
   period or a seat rationalizing work around an unobservable surface. Mitigation
   is discipline plus the mandated clerk/executive detection procedure; the
   blocker must be reported to the Founder, not worked around.
2. **Loss of the bright-line prohibition.** The old rule was a hard "never other
   projects." The new rule is visibility-based; if assignments are ambiguous or
   spoofable, scope creep is possible. The conservative default and the
   ambiguity-recording duty are the safeguards; the review must test them.
3. **Ambiguity about Viche's own self-governance.** The new Act 9 defines scope
   solely as Antonina issues assigned to `phoebe-dev`; it does not explicitly
   state that the Town's own constitutional self-government work (Art. V) — e.g.
   this very bill — remains in scope absent an assignment. I read self-government
   as constitutional and therefore always in scope, but the Act would be clearer
   with an explicit carve-out. Recommended amendment at review, not a reason to
   reject.

None of these is a reason to vote NO: the direction is the Founder's, the
implementation is faithful and conservative, and it is reviewable after three
restarts or seven days.
