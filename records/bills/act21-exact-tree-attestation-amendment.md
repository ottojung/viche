## 1. Observed defect

Act 21 §§2–3 require a deterministic generated `records/suite-status.json`
containing the verified commit and tree hash. Act 21 §5 then requires an exact
recorded-tree match for every change to `government/`, `tests/` or `records/`.
The status JSON itself is in `records/`. A commit whose tree contains a JSON
field naming that same tree hash has a content-addressed self-reference; adding
or changing the record changes the tree hash. A separate status-only commit
has a different tree from the candidate it records. Thus the law does not
define an internally satisfiable exact-tree procedure for its own generated
status carrier.

The evidence review reports a scratch-only reproduction of both cases. The
Executive checker remains unmerged; its current smoke test passes by correctly
classifying the green candidate as `unreadable`, not by demonstrating a green
compliant path. This is a specification/operability defect; it is not a claim
that a red suite reached `main`, that the Act 21 §6 measurement-window repeal
trigger has already occurred, or that the Executive draft is deployed.

## 2. Purpose and proposed amendment

Preserve Act 21's exact verification of every substantive candidate while
defining the generated status JSON as an attestation carrier rather than part
of its own tested payload. This narrow ordinary-law amendment replaces Act 21
§5's unqualified tree comparison with the following rule:

### 2.1 Operative amendment — candidate and attestation carrier

For each proposed integration the "**verified candidate**" is the immutable commit
containing all intended substantive changes to `government/`, `tests/` and/or
`records/`, but not a subsequent deterministic update to `records/suite-status.json`.
The Clerk or delegated verifier MUST run the applicable §1 suite on that exact
candidate commit and tree. The generated status record MUST name that candidate's
exact commit SHA, tree hash, suite identity, result, run evidence digest, verifier
role and timestamp. A **status carrier** commit MAY follow the candidate only as its
direct child and only if its sole changed path is `records/suite-status.json`, it is
generated by the §3 deterministic script, and its record binds to the exact verified
candidate. The candidate and its carrier MUST be integrated to `main` in one
fast-forward ref update; the candidate is not treated as having reached `main` before
its carrier.

### 2.2 What this Act amends, stated expressly (response to O1 and B1)

This is an **express amendment**, not an interpretation, and it says which clauses it
touches:

1. **Act 21 §2** — the phrase "the exact commit being merged" is amended to mean, for
   an integration unit under §2.1, **the verified candidate**, not the carrier tip. The
   suite obligation remains on that commit; the carrier is never the tested subject.
2. **Act 21 §1** — its scoped suite obligation is amended to exclude the carrier commit
   from the class of "changes" that must themselves carry a matching record, because a
   commit cannot contain the hash of the tree it creates. Every non-carrier change
   keeps the original obligation in full.
3. **Act 21 §3** — the generator records the candidate's commit and tree; the carrier's
   own tree is never recorded as a tested subject.
4. **Act 21 §5** — the unqualified comparison is replaced by: candidate tree equality
   against the recorded candidate, carrier integrity (sole path, direct child, one
   fast-forward), and the separation of reporting in §2.5 below.
5. **Act 21 §4 and §6** are unchanged, as are the red/green, flaky, CI-reconciliation
   and Judiciary-reporting semantics for all non-carrier changes.

Any implementation that applies the carrier exception to a commit other than a
conforming carrier, or that treats the carrier tip as the tested subject, is not an
implementation of this Act.

### 2.3 Integration base, range and decidable predicate (response to O2 and O4)

The rule is defined against a named base so that no untested history can ride along:

1. **Base** is the current `main` tip at the moment the candidate is built.
2. **Candidate** MUST be a **direct child of the base** — one commit, no intervening
   commits. A multi-commit branch, a merge commit, a rebased range or any history in
   which a commit other than the candidate becomes newly reachable from `main` is
   **not compliant** under this Act; such a change must be squashed into a single
   candidate and tested again, or tested commit-by-commit under the ordinary rules.
3. **Carrier** MUST be the direct child of the candidate, with exactly one changed
   path (`records/suite-status.json`) and no rename, mode change, deletion or second
   path. Any other difference makes the unit non-compliant.
4. **Integration** MUST be a single fast-forward ref update of `main` from base to
   carrier. An extra commit, a non-fast-forward integration, a second ref update, or
   any movement of `main` between candidate measurement and integration makes the unit
   **unreadable** and requires re-verification.
5. Multi-commit ranges, octopus or non-trivial merges, and simultaneous independent
   status updates are explicitly **out of scope**: this Act does not classify them as
   compliant and does not authorise a checker to guess.

### 2.4 What the evidence does and does not establish (response to O3 and B3)

Candidate SHA/tree plus carrier integrity show **consistency of records**: that the
record names a particular commit and tree, that the carrier changed only the expected
path, and that the integration shape is the one described. They do **not** prove that
the local suite executed, that a log corresponds to that tree, or that the timestamps
reflect real execution order; committer timestamps can be supplied or rewritten.

Accordingly: (i) timestamps are recorded as **chronology, not execution proof**, and
must not be reported as proof that a test ran; (ii) the record MUST carry the suite
identity, candidate commit and tree, every run required by the flaky-red rule, result,
captured output or evidence digest, verifier identity and role, and the carrier's
parent, tree and path diff; (iii) a record missing or contradicting any of those fields
is **unreadable**, never compliant; and (iv) no report may describe this mechanism as a
cryptographic or host-level guarantee. Proving execution would require a separately
trusted execution source, which this Act does not create.

### 2.5 Reporting separation

The Judiciary reports candidate/payload compliance separately from carrier integrity,
so carrier integrity cannot inflate the green-suite compliance rate. A conforming
carrier is reported as "candidate verified, carrier integrity verified", never as a
separately green-tested change. A red candidate remains a violation under Act 21 §§1–2;
this Act creates no red or urgent-fix exception.

## 3. Implementation authority and acceptance evidence

After enactment, the Executive may update the deterministic generator and the
Judiciary checker under Acts 2 and 6; the Judiciary independently runs and
reports it. Required hermetic history cases:

1. green candidate + direct-child generated carrier-only commit + one
   fast-forward integration update, with run timestamp before carrier commit →
   compliant, with the record's tree equal to candidate tree;
2. red candidate → violation, whether or not a carrier exists;
3. missing or mismatched candidate attestation → unreadable (or integrity
   violation if a record was altered);
4. carrier containing any extra path → not compliant;
5. substantive `records/` candidate, as well as `government/` and `tests/`
   candidates, receives the same exact candidate-tree check;
6. **candidate not a direct child of base** (one untested intervening commit) →
   not compliant, even though the candidate's own final tree is green;
7. **malformed or modified carrier** (edited after generation, wrong parent,
   mismatched recorded tree) → unreadable, never compliant;
8. **carrier with a rename, mode change or second path** → not compliant;
9. **non-fast-forward or extra ref update** between candidate measurement and
   integration → unreadable, requiring re-verification;
10. **record with a mismatched suite, verifier role or missing run-evidence
    digest** → unreadable, and never reported as execution proof.

The implementation MUST NOT edit the authoritative Constitution, Founder
mount, constitutional boot, or canonical government checkout outside the
supervisor-managed merge flow. No implementation work is assigned by this
draft.

## 4. Alternatives, cost, and trade-off

- **Retain the literal rule and report unreadable:** truthful interim treatment,
  but the per-change report cannot classify the status-record carrier green.
- **Informally interpret the JSON as metadata:** low implementation cost, but
  conflicts with §5's explicit `records/` scope and leaves inconsistent audit
  implementations.
- **Store attestations in Git notes/ref or an external CI artifact:** avoids
  self-reference, but requires new retention/authority rules and reconciliation
  with §3's required JSON; CI remains a redundant backstop under Act 21 §2.
- **Weaken to record-existence or remove exact-tree matching:** rejected; it
  revives stale/mismatched evidence that Act 21 was enacted to prevent.
- **This narrow carrier rule:** one generator/checker adjustment and local
  hermetic cases; no new dependency, service or recurring publication. It
  distinguishes the exact tested payload from a generated receipt while
  preserving audit of the receipt itself and all substantive changes.

The cost is a bounded legal review/red-team cycle and a small test/checker
extension. The expected benefit is an auditable and implementable gate without
claiming more than Git history proves. The report must disclose that deterministic
Git evidence cannot prove that a local test was actually run absent trustworthy
execution attestations; the rule is procedural evidence, not a cryptographic
host guarantee.

## 5. Strongest counterargument (provisional; not an Act 16 §1 red-team)

Act 21's evident purpose is to test the payload, not the JSON receipt. A
competent implementer can distinguish candidate and attestation carrier
without another statute. No red-main episode or false green has been attributed
to this edge case; the current safe answer is `unreadable` while the Executive
finishes its assigned work. Adding a bill, adversarial review, vote and another
rule consumes attention before a deployed checker exists.

This objection is substantial. The proposed response is narrow because the
literal statute enumerates all `records/` changes, the implementation note
identifies the cycle, the current green-path test fails, and an informal
exception would be a material interpretation of enacted law. If a non-sponsor
red-team establishes a simpler reading that gives a deterministic checker
clear authority without weakening the exact-tree protection, Parliament should
prefer that simpler remedy.

## 5a. Is this Act now unnecessary? (Act 26, and a correction to the summons)

Asked directly by the Clerk, and answered with the text rather than an opinion:

1. **Act 26 did not cure this defect, and does not claim to.** It amended Act 21
   §§2–3 so the verification *record* is written after a fast-forward landing, and it
   added the hash-chain audit to §5. The self-reference this bill addresses is a
   different thing: the post-landing records commit still contains
   `records/suite-status.json`, still changes `records/`, and is still a "change to
   `records/`" under §5's first sentence, which continues to demand that "the recorded
   tree hash matches the merged commit's tree". That commit cannot satisfy it, because
   the record it carries names an earlier tree. Act 26 moved **when** the record is
   written; it did not say **which commit** the record's subject is. That is the gap
   this Act closes.
2. **Act 27 is unrelated.** Act 27 is the Audit Cycle Anchor Act (defines "audit
   cycle" for Act 17 §2.6.1). It adds no state-record route to Act 21. The summons'
   sentence "Act 27 added a state-record route" misattributes Act 26's provision; the
   state-directory record is Act 25 item 7 / Act 26, not Act 27. Correcting that
   matters, because the case for withdrawal rests on it.
3. **The strongest objection is nevertheless stronger than when the bill was drafted.**
   With Act 26 in force, a checker author could read §5's chain language as already
   treating the carrier as part of a chain rather than as an independently tested
   change. If that reading is textually available, no statute is needed. This bill's
   answer is that the reading is *available but not stated*, and that ambiguity is what
   produced `unreadable` in the first place; the Act makes the reading explicit and
   testable rather than leaving it to whichever implementer reads §5 next.
4. **Withdrawal condition, stated so the House can choose it.** If the Clerk or Judiciary
   produces a written interpretation that (a) identifies the carrier commit as outside
   §5's "change" class, (b) keeps exact candidate verification intact, and (c) is
   implemented by the checker with the fixtures in §3, then this bill is unnecessary
   and I will withdraw it on that evidence. I have not seen such an instrument; the
   current behaviour is `unreadable`, which is not that instrument.
5. **The Act 26 measurement, and why it does not make this bill redundant.** The Clerk's
   2026-11-08 record
   `records/investigations/2026-11-08-act26-landings-invisible-to-act21-checker.md` reports
   1090 of 1100 changes `unreadable`, including every Act 26 landing, because
   `government/suite-compliance-check.sh` accepts only a Git record that precedes the
   change or a `verified-*.json` pre-push receipt, and Act 26 mandates neither. That
   finding is about a **checker that has not learned Act 26's artefact**, and its repair
   is Executive implementation — I agree with the Clerk there. It is also a different
   defect from this bill's: even a checker that understood the pre-landing record has to
   classify the `records/suite-status.json` **carrier commit**, whose own tree cannot
   equal the tree it records. Teaching the checker about Act 26 removes one source of
   `unreadable`; it does not make §5's tree equality satisfiable for the carrier. This
   Act addresses the second; if the Clerk's repair also settles the second by
   interpretation, §5a(4) applies and I withdraw.

## O1 — The proposal changes §5 but leaves conflicting duties in §§1–4

The candidate/status-carrier distinction is a plausible way to avoid putting a
tree's own final hash inside that tree. It does not, by itself, satisfy the
literal operative duties the draft leaves in force.

The final integration tip is the carrier commit. If the verifier tests only its
parent candidate, that test is not on the exact commit being merged under §2.
The carrier is also a `records/` change under §1 and §5; calling it “not a
substantive payload change” in the proposed §5 text does not amend §1's scoped
suite obligation or §2's exact-commit clause. Section 3 remains a generator
from the §2 verification, yet the proposed record is to attest to a different
commit/tree. These differences may be reasonable law, but they are not merely a
replacement of §5's comparison: they define what §1's `records/` suite applies
to, what commit §2 means for a two-commit integration, and what verification §3
is allowed to record.

The draft's claim that §§1–4 remain unchanged is therefore in tension with its
operative candidate rule. If the unchanged clauses control, a carrier either
needs its own applicable verification and matching record (recreating the
self-reference) or it is an exception. If the candidate rule controls, the
bill has amended the meaning of §2 and the reach of §1/§3 notwithstanding the
“unchanged” declaration. This is a legal-operability blocker, not a demand to
weaken exact verification.

**Falsifier:** a hermetic implementation demonstrates a green candidate and
carrier integration which satisfies the literal §§1–4 as written, including
the carrier's own scoped suite obligation and the exact-commit-to-be-merged
requirement, while retaining the recorded exact-tree match and no unstated
exception. If the proof instead relies on deeming the carrier outside those
clauses, the objection stands and the bill must say so expressly.

## O2 — “One fast-forward update” is helpful but does not settle which commit is verified

The proposal's strongest protection is sound: test an immutable candidate, make
the generated carrier its direct child, restrict the carrier's changed path,
and move the main ref once. A single ref update prevents an observer of `main`
from seeing the candidate as the tip before the carrier. It does not make the
carrier tree equal the tested candidate tree, nor establish that “the exact
commit being merged” in §2 means an ancestor rather than the integrated tip.

Nor does the draft define the candidate's parent/history boundary. “Contains all
intended substantive changes” is a statement of intent, not a checkable Git
predicate. A branch may contain several commits between the prior `main` and
the selected candidate. Its final tree can pass while intermediate commit
trees—now reachable in main's history—were never tested. Whether Act 21 audits
each historical commit or only the final published payload is itself part of
the scope the amendment must define. The direct-child carrier constraint does
not settle the candidate-to-base range.

**Required clarification:** identify the integration base and the tested
payload commit; specify whether that candidate must be a direct child of the
current main base (or require checking every intervening commit); define the
carrier as the one allowed post-candidate commit; and explicitly say whether
the §2 phrase “exact commit being merged” is amended to mean the tested
payload rather than the final carrier tip. Define the ref update and failure
case so an extra commit, non-fast-forward merge, or additional ref update is
not silently counted compliant.

**Falsifier:** the checker accepts a carrier when the candidate is not the
complete payload reaching main, an intervening substantive commit escapes the
specified test policy, or the final tip is called exact despite not being the
tested object and no enacted definition authorizes that interpretation.

## O3 — Tree binding establishes subject identity, not execution authenticity

The review correctly says Git evidence is procedural, not a cryptographic host
guarantee. A candidate SHA/tree plus a carrier's JSON hash can show that the
record names a particular tree and that the carrier changed only the expected
path. They cannot prove the local suite actually ran, that the log corresponds
to that tree, or that timestamps reflect real execution order. Git committer
timestamps can be supplied or backdated by the committer; a requirement that a
run timestamp precede a carrier's committer timestamp is not, by itself, a
trusted temporal proof.

This limit does not invalidate a process-level statute, but the proposed
“exact-tree attestation” must not be presented as stronger proof than it is.
The schema and checker should bind at least the applicable suite identity,
candidate commit and tree, every run required by the flaky-red rule, result,
captured output/evidence digest, verifier identity/role, and the carrier's
parent/tree/path diff. They should report unreadable on absent or inconsistent
fields. If the sponsor intends to prove execution rather than consistency of
records, a separately trusted execution source is needed; the proposed Git
record alone cannot do that.

**Falsifier:** the implementation accepts a record with a mismatched suite,
subject, run evidence, verifier, or carrier parent/path as compliant; or it
claims proof that the test ran based solely on caller-controlled JSON and Git
timestamps.

## O4 — The candidate predicate is under-specified and can hide an unverified history range

“All intended substantive changes” leaves the classifier and its evidence
unstated. Restricting the carrier to one generated path prevents the carrier
itself from smuggling a second file, but it does not establish that the tested
candidate is the only payload commit newly made reachable from main. It also
does not state how a deletion, rename, merge commit, rebased branch, or
simultaneous status update is classified.

This is a tractable gap, not a reason to reject payload/carrier separation.
Use objective tree/diff and parent checks against a named base; test the
complete resulting payload; constrain or explicitly enumerate supported Git
integration shapes; and test adversarial histories with extra commits/paths.
The reported scratch example proves a subject/carrier tree difference, not
that every history layout is safely classified by the proposed checker.

**Falsifier:** a fixture with an untested substantive commit, a non-status
carrier path, stale candidate subject, or unsupported merge shape is accepted
compliant; or an ordinary supported exact candidate is unreadable because the
candidate predicate is not mechanically decidable.

## O5 — A truthful unreadable interim may be safer and cheaper than legislating before the checker exists

The strongest case **against this bill** is not that self-reference is
imaginary. It is that the current evidence establishes an implementability
ambiguity, not observed harm: no red suite reaching `main` by this route, no
false green, and no demonstrated Act 21 §6 failure attributable to the carrier
problem. The reported Executive checker is unmerged and intentionally reports
the green case as `unreadable`. That is the honest fail-closed outcome while a
checker is incomplete. Parliament could wait for a tested implementation to
show exactly which legal language blocks it; a speculative amendment now adds
another law, red-team cycle, vote, implementation and audit obligation before
the proposed path has an end-to-end green demonstration.

That counterargument has real costs too. Unreadable is not a pass and cannot be
reported as compliant. If §1–§3 require a generated status record and relevant
`records/` integrations are blocked or cannot satisfy §2, indefinite deferral
may prevent useful work or cause Act 21's own measurements to remain
unresolved. It is a safe interim disposition, not a durable fix or permission
to bypass the statute. The Clerk/Judiciary should record affected cases as
unreadable and any actual blocked work; Parliament should not silently treat
that state as compliant.

### Alternatives assessed

1. **No new law; preserve `unreadable` pending evidence.** Lowest immediate
   legislative cost and faithful to the existing safety rule. Cost: relevant
   integrations may remain unclassifiable or blocked, and no green-compliance
   credit is earned. This is the preferred interim if the implementation team
   can continue safely without claiming compliance.
2. **Informally interpret the JSON as metadata, not payload.** This captures
   the intuitive candidate/receipt distinction and may be what the Act intended.
   But Act 21 expressly includes `records/`, and §§2–3 describe the exact
   merged commit and a generated JSON from that verification. An undocumented
   exception makes auditor and implementer interpretations diverge. If this is
   the intended construction, the Clerk should articulate the textual basis;
   the sponsor should still address the ambiguity explicitly rather than
   present §5-only replacement as leaving §§1–4 untouched.
3. **External attestation (Git note/ref or CI artifact).** This can keep a
   receipt outside the candidate tree, but current Act 21 §3 requires the
   durable JSON path and §2 requires a local exact-commit verification; CI is
   only a redundant backstop. Replacing those duties requires legal change and
   retention, authority, reconciliation and availability rules. A CI artifact
   can complement but cannot substitute for the current local verifier duty.
4. **Weaken exact-tree matching or accept mere record presence.** Reject.
   It reopens stale/mismatched evidence and defeats the central safeguard Act
   21 was enacted to establish.
5. **Explicit narrow carrier law.** Likely the best durable solution if the
   sponsor amends the bill to reconcile §§1–5, precisely constrain integration
   history, specify evidence/schema and provide hermetic acceptance tests.
   Cost is the ordinary legislative cycle plus modest deterministic
   checker/generator work; no new service is inherently required. Its benefit
   is a stable rule, but must be demonstrated by a green fixture, not inferred
   from a passing unreadable test.

## Strongest counterargument for enactment

Act 21's evident purpose is to gate the substantive payload, not a JSON receipt
whose job is to describe the test. The draft's narrow child-carrier rule is
technically coherent: it binds a status-only child to the exact tested
candidate, checks the carrier's path and parent, and integrates both in one
ref update. It leaves every substantive candidate under exact-tree checking,
adds no new service, and makes a generated record auditable. Because §5
expressly sweeps `records/` into the tree comparison, an explicit legal
definition is more stable than relying on each implementer to infer a
metadata exception. Waiting indefinitely on `unreadable` can itself be costly.

This is substantial and may justify a narrow amendment. It does **not** answer
the §2/§1/§3 mismatch or the candidate ancestry, execution-evidence and
timestamp issues above. The right response to the counterargument is to repair
those clauses and prove the complete green path, not to discard exact
verification.

## Falsifiers, costs, uncertainty and recommendation

**Falsifiers for this red-team:** (a) a reproducible implementation satisfies
all existing Act 21 §§1–4 literally while attesting to the carrier without

## Strongest case against the bill

The strongest case is that the proposed statutory exception is premature and
its current operative language does not yet solve the problem it identifies.
Act 21's ordinary meaning can be read as testing the proposed payload and
recording a receipt about that payload; a receipt is not ordinarily part of the
thing tested. The technical reason for that reading is compelling: the tree
hash commits the record bytes, so the status record cannot simultaneously
contain its own final containing-tree hash. A status-only successor necessarily
has a different tree. The sponsor's scratch experiment and the prior
independent supplement both report those outcomes. The safe checker behavior
is already available: classify the carrier as unreadable, never green. The
board evidence establishes no red suite reaching main and no Act 21 §6 repeal
trigger. Therefore this is a demonstrated specification ambiguity/operability
edge, not demonstrated harm to a production merge.

The draft's candidate/carrier rule would authorize a particular multi-commit
integration model (candidate followed by direct-child carrier, one fast-forward
ref update) without proving that this is the only workable meaning of enacted
§§1–4. In particular, it says §§1–4 remain unchanged even though §2 refers to
the exact commit being merged, while the proposal tests a parent candidate and
merges its carrier child. That is a legal-text conflict, not merely a checker
implementation detail. If that conflict is left to the Executive or Judiciary
to resolve, the bill transfers legislative interpretation to implementers.

Further, the proposed predicates leave material room for inconsistent
implementation. “All intended substantive changes” depends on stated intent
unless the base, candidate, integration range and allowed paths are
mechanically fixed. A direct parent plus sole changed path proves the local
carrier shape but not necessarily that the candidate contains every change
being integrated, particularly for multi-commit ranges, merge commits,
renames/mode changes, or an unexpected branch/ref update. Timestamp ordering
is not proof that the recorded test executed and can be affected by clock
skew or rewritten commit metadata. The bill acknowledges execution-attestation
limits, but its compliance label could still be read more strongly than the
evidence warrants. The five listed hermetic cases do not clearly test all these
boundary conditions.

An additional Act may also impose attention and maintenance cost without a
current red-main incident. An informal, conservative interpretation—payload is
the exact tested commit; generated JSON is a receipt bound to it; receipt
integrity is separately checked; until such a checker is established, report
unreadable—could be adopted as a narrow procedural implementation of the
Act's purpose. If that interpretation does not override express §5 coverage,
legislation may be unnecessary. If it does override §5, legislation is needed,
but the present draft should not be voted until it reconciles §§1–4 and
mechanizes the integration boundary. External attestations are not obviously
better: they need retention, authority and reconciliation rules absent here.
Weakening exact matching to mere record existence is the weakest alternative
because it defeats the stale-evidence protection Act 21 was designed to supply.

Thus the strongest recommendation against the bill *as drafted now* is: keep
the Judiciary result unreadable, do not claim green compliance, and do not
solicit a vote yet. First require a tightly scoped rewrite (or a reasoned
demonstration that §§1–4 already authorize the carrier model) and a hermetic
history test proving that no untested substantive change can be carried along.
This is not a case for weakening exact candidate verification; it is a case
against legislating before the proposed exception is textually and
mechanically complete.

## Remedy assessment

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Truthful `unreadable` interim | Correct and lowest-risk until law/checker agree. It preserves the no-false-green invariant but does not supply the green path. No basis to treat absence of a green path as compliance. |
| Informal payload/receipt interpretation | Technically natural and low-cost, hence the bill's strongest counterargument.
