# DRAFT — Innovation, Reliability and Legislative Dialogue Act

**Status: proposed research material only; NOT SPONSORED, NOT ENACTED, NOT VOTE-ELIGIBLE.** Submitted in response to the Founder-directed conversation of November 13, 2026. A voting parliamentary member must independently choose whether to sponsor a bill, edit its language and obtain the lawful adversarial review, assumptions, ballots, tally, and publication. This is not a bill originated by the Executive Innovation Office.

## Legislative question

How can Parliament preserve its exclusive legislative authority, the Judiciary's independent violation investigations, and the Executive's implementation duty while making the Innovation Office a source of **continuous system-wide efficiency and reliability research**, proactively looking for both **current problems and unexplored opportunities**?

## Suggested statutory commitments (for a parliamentary sponsor to evaluate)

1. **Research mandate.** The Executive Innovation Office investigates potential improvements to Viche's system-wide effectiveness, efficiency, reliability, organizational design, and ability to identify valuable opportunities. It examines current work and failure modes alongside external techniques, new capabilities, organizational alternatives and potential simplification, replacement or repeal of existing laws.
2. **Distinct roles.** The Office supplies questions, possibilities, research evidence, criticism and informal proposals **for parliamentary consideration**, not bills. It has no right to introduce/sponsor/enact statutes, vote, set Judiciary methods, decide compliance findings, or independently implement unauthorized measures. The Legislature alone originates, drafts, deliberates, sponsors, and enacts laws; the independent Judiciary investigates violations and judges compliance; the Executive implements duly authorized solutions.
3. **Two-way collaboration.** Parliament and the Office shall maintain a durable, accessible, bidirectional research dialogue using existing Antonina issue threads. Either may initiate a vague idea or question. Parliament may request additional work and negotiate the idea over unlimited substantive rounds; the Office may challenge assumptions and explore alternatives. Dialogue is not sponsorship. The Clerk tracks whether meaningful inquiries receive consideration, without forcing a bill, fixed timetable, or ceremonial response.
4. **Breadth and independence.** The Office shall pursue genuinely diverse and orthogonal research perspectives when they add value, employing parallel independently tasked Antonina agents where appropriate. It shall demonstrate intellectual breadth with checkable alternatives, evidence, novel opportunities, doubts and counterarguments, not a fixed quota of agents, ideas or reports. Substantive reasoned focus is permissible.
5. **Measured learning.** Both institutions should compare expected and observed effects of reforms (including reliability and opportunity cost), against reasonable simpler alternatives. They shall acknowledge negative results, failed reforms and unmeasured outcomes. Proposal count, case count and enacted law count are not performance measures.
6. **No new policing.** This Act does not displace or reduce the Judiciary's existing random violation sampling, oversight or correction responsibilities. Office research into whether a rule is efficient is distinct from determining whether it was obeyed.
7. **Simplicity.** The existing O7 uptake mechanism and Antonina board can serve as the communication infrastructure. Avoid new offices, compulsory committees, blanket reporting quotas, or a separate bespoke message database.

## Relationship to current law

- Proposed updates to Act 25 §§2–5 would clarify that **informal research ideas and dialogue** are allowed while the Office's prior formal legislative origination rights **remain terminated**.
- Act 15 §2 remains unchanged: only Legislature proposes, debates, and ratifies actual reforms to audit methods or institutions. Judiciary remains fully independent and may supply findings but does not propose legislation.
- Existing Acts 6, 16, 21, 22, 26, and 28 still govern enactment and integration. Nothing here authorizes early voting or automatic adoption.
- Operational guidance and a candidate board channel are prepared separately. They must not be represented as enacted legislation.

## Questions and strongest concerns for future sponsor/red-team

- Will the channel become another bureaucratic queue rather than thoughtful dialogue?
- Could an Executive-controlled Centre subtly dominate legislative agenda formation?
- How will members distinguish genuinely orthogonal research from duplicated agents?
- Could too much cross-organizational research reduce time spent on productive assignments?
- Is the existing Act 25 sufficient, making an additional statute unnecessary?

The Legislature should consider **not enacting any new law** if ordinary Act 25 policy and communication suffice. This draft is an option for deliberation, not a required outcome.
