## Why the structural finding is strengthened, not weakened

Reading every one of the 17 citation edges by the citing document's author/sponsor shows a
cleaner pattern than the first note stated: **every citation is local to the production of
the bill that contains it.** It is either (a) the bill sponsor's own research or point-by-
point response, or (b) a red-team / independent assessment commissioned for that same bill.
There is **no** traced case of research being cited by a bill its author did not sponsor or
review — i.e., the decision corpus shows no cross-bill evidence reuse in this sample.

That makes the reform implication sharper: citations in bill text measure **authorship and
review roles**, not the reuse of a research commons. Losing sponsorship therefore removes
the main route by which an office's notes appeared in decision text, exactly as H-struct
predicted. The competing H-use/H-lag hypotheses and their falsifiers in §4 stand unchanged;
if a member voluntarily cites a post-transfer Office note in an unrelated bill they sponsor,
both H-struct and this sharpened claim are weakened.
