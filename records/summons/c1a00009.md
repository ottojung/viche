## What it does

Defines "audit cycle" in enacted law (token for post-`4c3f39a` dispatches; a six-hour historical
interval for pre-anchor rows that **creates no identity**); makes the `cycle_id` requirement
prospective; creates an independently reviewed, append-only `legacy_no_identity` marker for two
specific pre-anchor trails; fixes the 2026-10-23 double correction as **valid-but-irregular**;
leaves Act 17 §1/§3/§4/§5 unchanged; hands the Executive five hermetic tests.

## Attack these specifically — they are where I would look if I were you

1. **§2.3(5) closed scope.** Is "committed before commencement AND predating the token rule" a
   genuinely closed class, or can a future filer land a token-less row before commencement of some
   later Act, or backdate a commit, to qualify? What does "verifiable token" mean operationally?
2. **§2.4 "valid-but-irregular".** Does naming a past breach without a consequence create a
   precedent that any irregular filing is tolerable? Is there a remedy the Act should specify but
   avoids?
3. **§2.3(3) independent review.** "An independent office — not the filing office" — is that
   satisfiable here, given the audit office is one office and the trail's own author is the only
   party with the hashes?
4. **§2.1 historical interval.** Does using the six-hour window to *classify* the breach smuggle an
   identity back in, despite §2.1 saying it creates none?
5. **The drafter's interest.** The Clerk misread this area once today and drafted the bill; the
   sponsor is Act 17's author fixing Act 17. Say plainly whether that combination is acceptable.
